Your first step before buying a home (it isn't looking at houses)

Most people start on Zillow. The buyers who get the right house at the right price start somewhere else.

Almost everyone starts the same way: scrolling listings at night, saving a few favorites, maybe going to an open house. It's fun, and it's backwards. You fall for houses before you know what you can buy, and the first real conversation about money happens after your heart is already set.

Here is the order I walk every buyer through. None of it costs anything, and most of it takes an evening.

1. Write down your why and your when

Two sentences. Why are you moving, and when do you need to be in? "Our lease ends in June and we want a yard for the kids" tells me more than a list of 20 features. Your timeline decides how patient we can be and how hard we push in a competitive offer.

2. Pick a monthly payment, not a price

Lenders approve you for a maximum. That number is not a recommendation. Decide what monthly payment you're comfortable with after daycare, car payments and savings, and work backward from there. Remember the payment includes property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA dues, not just the loan.

3. Count your cash

You need money for three things:

  • Down payment. It depends on the loan. Some programs allow a small down payment, and Minnesota has first-time buyer and down payment assistance programs worth asking a lender about.
  • Closing costs. Lender fees, title, prepaid taxes and insurance. Your lender will give you an estimate.
  • A cushion. Something will break in the first year. Keep money aside for it so a water heater doesn't become a credit card balance.

4. Talk to a lender and get pre-approved

A pre-approval means a lender has looked at your income, credit and assets and put a number in writing. Sellers expect one with every offer. It also surfaces problems early, like a credit report error, while there's still time to fix them. Talk to more than one lender and compare their loan estimates side by side.

5. Choose two or three towns

Drive them on a weekday at rush hour and on a Saturday. Look at commute, schools if they matter to you, and what a typical house in your price range actually looks like there. It's easier to find the right house in the right town than to talk yourself into the wrong one.

6. Then call me

Bring the pre-approval, your two sentences and your towns. In one conversation we'll turn that into a search, set up alerts for new listings, and agree on what a "yes" house looks like so we can move fast when it shows up.
