Buying a home in Minnesota, start to finish: what it actually looks like

From the first call to the keys in your hand. What happens, roughly how long it takes, and what you do at each stage.

People tell me the hardest part of buying a house is not knowing what comes next. So here is the whole thing, in order. Every deal is a little different, but the shape is always the same.

Stage 1: Getting ready

You: get pre-approved, decide on a monthly payment you're comfortable with, and pick a few towns. (I wrote a whole post on this first step.)

Me: a planning conversation. We agree on must-haves, nice-to-haves and deal breakers, and I set up a search that sends you new listings the day they hit the market.

Stage 2: Seeing homes

This can take one weekend or a few months, depending on the market and how specific you are. At showings I'm looking past the paint: roof age, furnace and water heater age, signs of water in the basement, grading around the foundation, and anything that will cost money in the next few years. You'll hear it from me before you fall in love.

Tip: take a photo of the front of each house and one note about it. After the fourth showing, they blur together.

Stage 3: Writing the offer

When you find the one, I pull recent sales of similar homes nearby so the price has a reason behind it. Then we build the rest of the offer:

  • Price, based on comparable sales and how much competition the house has.
  • Earnest money, a good-faith deposit held by the title company and credited to you at closing.
  • Contingencies, like inspection and financing, that let you back out for specific reasons.
  • Closing date that lines up with your lease or your current home.

The seller can accept, counter or reject. I talk to the listing agent before and after we submit so we know where we stand.

Stage 4: Inspection

Once the offer is accepted, the clock starts. You hire an inspector, usually within the first few days, and I'm there for it. The report will be long. We sort it into three piles: safety and structure, systems near the end of their life, and normal maintenance. Then we decide what, if anything, to ask the seller to fix or credit. (More on how I read an inspection report.)

Stage 5: Appraisal and loan approval

Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the house is worth what you're paying, and underwriting finishes reviewing your file. Your job here is simple and important: send documents quickly, and don't open new credit, buy a car or change jobs until after closing.

From accepted offer to closing often runs about 30 to 45 days with a mortgage. Cash can be faster.

Stage 6: Final walkthrough and closing

Within a day or so of closing, we walk the house to confirm it's in the condition you agreed to and any promised repairs are done. Then you sign at the title company, the funds move, and you get the keys. Bring your ID and wire money only after confirming instructions by phone with the title company directly.

Stage 7: After the keys

  • File for homestead with your county so you're taxed as an owner-occupant.
  • Change the locks and the garage code.
  • Keep my number. Questions about contractors, property taxes or a strange noise in the furnace are part of the job.
