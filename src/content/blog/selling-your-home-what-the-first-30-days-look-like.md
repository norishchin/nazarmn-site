Most of a sale is decided before the first showing. A house that launches at the right price, clean and well photographed, gets the most attention in its first week, which is when the best offers come. A house that launches wrong spends weeks being discounted. Here is how I run the month before.

## Week 1: Walk-through and pricing

I walk the house with you and ask what you've updated and when. Then I pull recent sales, active competition, and homes that sat without selling. You get a recommended price with the reasoning written down, not a number picked to win the listing.

## Week 2: The prep list that pays

You get a short list, ranked by what the money returns. Usually that means:

- Fix what an inspector will flag: dripping faucets, loose handrails, missing outlet covers, a slow drain.
- Paint where walls are scuffed or loud colors will distract buyers.
- Declutter hard. Pack a third of what's on shelves and counters now. You're moving anyway.
- Clean everything, including windows and the furnace filter.

What I usually tell people to skip: a big remodel right before listing. You rarely get the money back, and buyers want to pick their own finishes.

## Week 3: Photos and the listing

Professional photos, and drone shots if the lot or the view sells the house. I write the listing description myself, built around what makes your house different, not a list of adjectives. (If you're selling in spring, [shoot the outside in fall](/blog/selling-in-spring-take-photos-in-fall/).)

## Week 4: Launch

We pick a launch day that catches the weekend. Showings get scheduled around your life, and you get feedback after each one.

Want to know what you'll walk away with? Here's [what it costs to sell a house in Minnesota](/blog/cost-to-sell-a-house-in-minnesota/).

## When offers come in

I lay out each offer side by side: price, financing, earnest money, contingencies, and closing date. The highest number isn't always the best offer. A clean offer from a strong buyer can be worth more than a bigger one that falls apart at the appraisal.
