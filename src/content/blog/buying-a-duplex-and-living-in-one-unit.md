Living in one unit of a duplex, triplex or fourplex while renting the others is one of the best ways to start investing. You get a home and a rental in one purchase, and you can use a loan built for owner-occupants instead of an investor loan.

## Why it works

Investor loans usually need 20% to 25% down. Owner-occupant loans need much less, because you live there. The tenants' rent then covers part of your payment, so you build equity in a building while paying less than the full mortgage yourself.

## The loans people use

- **FHA.** As little as 3.5% down on a 1 to 4 unit home you live in. For 3 and 4 unit buildings, the rents usually have to pass a self-sufficiency test, meaning the building's expected rent has to cover the payment.
- **Conventional.** Some programs allow as little as 5% down on a 2 to 4 unit home you live in.
- **VA.** Eligible veterans and service members can buy a 1 to 4 unit home with no down payment if they live in one unit.

Lenders often count part of the expected rent from the other units toward your income. Rules change, so ask a lender which programs fit you before you shop. All of these loans expect you to actually move in, usually for at least a year.

## A worked example

These numbers are made up for illustration, not a quote, and the rate is just an assumption.

**The building:** a $400,000 duplex. You live in one side and rent the other for $1,500 a month.

**Your monthly cost of ownership with FHA at 3.5% down ($14,000) and 7%:**

- Principal and interest: about $2,568
- Mortgage insurance: about $177
- Property taxes: $450
- Homeowners insurance: $200

That's about $3,395 a month. FHA also has an upfront mortgage insurance fee, usually added to the loan.

**What the other unit brings in:** $1,500 rent, minus 5% for empty months ($75) and 8% for repairs ($120), so about $1,305.

**What it costs you to live there:** about $2,090 a month. Compare that with your rent today. When you move out later, both units are rentals and you already own the building.

## What to check before you buy

- **The leases and the tenants.** Ask for the rent roll, current leases and deposits. You inherit them at closing.
- **Separate utilities.** Separate meters for electric and gas save you money and arguments. Owner-paid water and heat add up.
- **The city's rental rules.** Many Minnesota cities require a rental license and inspection. Check before you count that rent.
- **Big-ticket items.** Roof, boiler or furnaces, water heaters and the sewer line. In a multi-unit building, each one costs more. The [inspection guide](/blog/what-i-look-for-in-an-inspection-report/) has what I look for first.
- **Your exit.** Will it still cash flow at market rent for both units after you move out? If not, it's a home with a roommate, which can still be fine, but know which one you're buying.
