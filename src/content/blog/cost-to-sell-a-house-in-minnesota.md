Most sellers know the price they want. Fewer know what's left after closing. Here is what comes out of the sale, in roughly the order you'll see it on the settlement statement.

## Agent commission

Commissions aren't set by law. They're negotiated and written into your listing agreement before your home goes on the market. Whether and how you help with the buyer's agent fee is a separate decision we make together, and it can be part of how you compete for buyers. Ask any agent you interview to walk you through exactly what they charge and what you get for it.

## Minnesota state deed tax

This one surprises people moving from other states. Minnesota charges a deed tax when the property changes hands, and the standard purchase agreement has the seller pay it.

- **Most of Minnesota:** 0.33% of the sale price
- **Hennepin and Ramsey counties:** 0.34% total (the 0.33% state rate plus a 0.01% Environmental Response Fund tax)

On a $450,000 sale, that's $1,485, or $1,530 in Eden Prairie, Hopkins, Plymouth or anywhere else in Hennepin County. The rate is set by the state, so it isn't negotiable. ([Minnesota Department of Revenue: deed tax rate](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/deed-tax-rate))

## Title and closing fees

The title company handles the closing. Your side usually includes a closing fee, deed preparation, recording costs for releasing your mortgage, and any work needed to show you can deliver clear title. Ask your title company for an estimate once you have an accepted offer.

## Your mortgage payoff and anything else attached to the house

Your lender sends a payoff amount good through the closing date. It's usually a little higher than your last statement because it includes interest up to that day. Home equity lines, liens and judgments get paid off at closing too.

## Prorated property taxes and special assessments

Property taxes are split so you pay for the days you owned the home that year. Special assessments (from street or utility projects, for example) are handled the way the purchase agreement says, so it's worth knowing about any pending ones before you list.

## Repairs and credits after the inspection

Most buyers ask for something after their inspection. You can fix it, give a credit at closing, lower the price, or say no. A short, reasonable request is normal. A house that's priced right and has the obvious fixes done before listing tends to get smaller requests. ([What the 30 days before you list look like](/blog/selling-your-home-what-the-first-30-days-look-like/).)

## City point-of-sale inspections

Some cities, including Minneapolis and St. Paul, require a point-of-sale inspection report before you list. It costs a few hundred dollars and can turn up items the city wants fixed. If your town has one, order it early.

## Getting ready and moving

Cleaning, touch-up paint, small repairs, storage and the move itself. These are smaller, but they add up, so put a number on them.

## Taxes on your gain

If you owned and lived in the home for at least two of the last five years, federal tax law lets you exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly. Rentals and investment property work differently. Talk to a tax professional about your situation. ([IRS Topic 701: sale of your home](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc701))
