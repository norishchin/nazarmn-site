When a good house gets several offers, the seller isn't just picking the biggest number. They're picking the offer most likely to close, on time, without surprises. Here's how to be that offer.

## Decide your top price before you write anything

Look at the recent sales I pull, decide the most you'd pay and still be glad you bought it, and write it down. Bidding wars move fast, and it's easy to talk yourself past a number you'd regret. If the house goes above it, the next one is usually a better use of your money.

## Know what the seller is comparing

- **Price.** It matters, but it's only one line.
- **Financing.** A strong pre-approval from a lender the listing agent can reach on the phone beats a letter from a website. Cash or a larger down payment helps because there's less that can go wrong.
- **Earnest money.** A larger deposit signals you're serious. It's credited to you at closing.
- **Inspection terms.** An inspection is your best protection, so I don't usually suggest skipping it. Some buyers keep the inspection but limit what they'll ask for, or agree to only walk away for major issues. That reads much stronger to a seller than a long repair list.
- **Appraisal.** If the price goes above what the appraiser says it's worth, the lender only lends on the appraised value. Saying up front that you'll cover some or all of that gap, if you can, is one of the strongest terms you can offer.
- **Closing date and possession.** Matching the seller's timeline, or letting them stay a few days after closing, can matter more to them than a few thousand dollars.

## Escalation clauses

An escalation clause says you'll beat the highest other offer by a set amount, up to a cap. It can work, but it shows the seller your ceiling and not every seller accepts it. I'll tell you whether it fits the situation, based on what the listing agent says.

## What to skip

- **Personal letters to the seller.** They can create fair housing problems for the seller, and many listing agents won't pass them on.
- **Waiving protections you can't afford to lose.** If a $15,000 surprise would sink you, keep the inspection contingency and keep your appraisal promise to an amount you actually have.

## Talk to the listing agent first

Before we write, I call the listing agent. Often I learn what the seller cares about most: a quick close, a rent-back, certainty over price. Writing the offer around that is the cheapest way to win.

## Have everything ready before the house is

Get fully pre-approved, have your down payment and earnest money where you can move them, and know your top price.
